Lena's future-state journey with the Riverty card

Lena, 32, Cologne: salaried, phone-first, no cushion. She uses BNPL on most non-trivial purchases and wants money to leave her account only after she has decided to keep the item. Today, on payday, she opens Klarna, PayPal and Riverty one after another and adds up what's due in her head.

Internal state is a hypothesis for interviews to confirm, not a measured result.

  1. 1. Sign up

    Wary

    User action
    Applies in the app -> gets a quick decision without a hard credit check.
    Internal state
    Wondering whether this is another card to forget.
    Pain point addressed
    Fear of surprise fees and hard credit checks.

  2. 2. Pay anywhere

    Reassured

    User action
    Taps card online or in store -> Riverty works where no BNPL button appears.
    Shops at partner merchants -> gets card benefits there (hypothesis, untested).
    Internal state
    No need to switch apps or fall back to debit.
    Pain point addressed
    Riverty missing at non-partner checkouts and the till.

  3. 3. Decide

    In control

    User action
    Orders several items, keeps some -> pays only for what she keeps.
    Internal state
    Deciding without the money already gone.
    Pain point addressed
    Paying before deciding; the "decide first, pay after" habit breaks.

  4. 4. Payday

    Calm

    User action
    Opens one Riverty view -> sees card spend due without mental arithmetic.
    Internal state
    Less to add up herself.
    Pain point addressed
    Manual payday tally across providers.

Three advantages over the manual workaround

  1. Ready-made total for card spend -> no mental arithmetic on payday.
  2. Works beyond partner checkouts -> no debit fallback at the till.
  3. Record updates itself with every purchase -> no stale notes or calendar entries.

What this future state assumes

  • Stage 3 is the biggest assumption. The brief never describes how a card could hold payment until she decides to keep an item. Interchange, refunds, credit policy and regulatory approval are all undefined.
  • Stage 1 depends on credit policy and regulatory approval allowing a soft-check decision.
  • Stage 2 in store depends on wallet access. The Apple pay-later slot is currently blocked by CCD2. The merchant benefit is a flagged hypothesis.
  • Stage 4 covers Riverty spend only. Klarna and PayPal balances stay separate unless Lena moves spend onto the card. The synthetic research put that switch at 29%.
  • Evidence base: the case brief and a synthetic UXR written for the exercise. No bug reports or real user data were used.
